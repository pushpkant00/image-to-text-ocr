Three steps, two seconds

Everything happens in your browser except the recognition call itself.

  1. 1

    Capture

    Press Ctrl+Shift+X and drag a box over anything — an image, a chart, a scanned PDF, a video frame. Or right-click an image, or paste a screenshot straight into the popup.

  2. 2

    Recognize

    The crop is resized and JPEG-encoded on your device, then sent over HTTPS to the provider you chose with your key. Text streams back while the model writes it.

  3. 3

    Copy

    Read it in the result card, copy with one click, find it again in your history, or export it as a Word or PDF file.

Everything you need, nothing you don't

Small extension, tight scope: get text out of images fast and get out of the way.

Drag-to-select OCR

Press the shortcut and draw a box over any region of any normal website. Video frames and PDF viewers included.

Right-click any image

A context-menu item turns the image under your cursor into selectable text.

Paste a screenshot

Copy a screenshot, open the popup and hit Ctrl+V. No file dialog, no upload page.

Live streaming results

Text appears in the card as the model writes it, so a slow provider still feels responsive.

Convert to Word or PDF

Jpg → Word, Pdf → Text, Pdf → Word, Text → PDF and Text → Word — all assembled locally.

Searchable history

Every result is stored as text with its language and timestamp. Search, filter, copy or delete from the side panel or the dashboard.

Optional encrypted sync

Sign in and your history follows you across devices. Text is encrypted on your device (AES-256-GCM) before it is uploaded.

Dark mode

Settings, history and the popup follow your system theme, with a manual toggle when you want to override it.

Bring your own key

There is no bundled engine and no developer server. You connect the extension to an OCR provider with your own free API key, and requests go straight from your browser to them.

Your images stay yours

No analytics, no ads, no tracking, and no developer server in the middle of your OCR.

Images go to one place

The capture is sent only to the OCR provider you selected, over HTTPS, with your key. The developer never sees it.

Keys stay local

Your API key lives in browser storage on this device. It is never synced, never logged, and never sent anywhere else.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. The extension itself is free and MIT licensed, and it has no paid tier. OCR runs on a free API key you get from Google AI Studio or OpenRouter, so the only limits are the provider's own free-tier quotas.

Do I need an API key?

One free key is all it takes. Open Settings, pick a provider and model, paste the key, click Save, then Test. It takes about a minute, and the key is stored only on your device.

Does it work offline?

Recognition does not — it is a cloud model, and the image has to reach the provider. Everything else is local: cropping, PDF text extraction, Word and PDF export, and your history. Google and OpenRouter both offer their own free offline tiers if you need one.

Which languages are supported?

English, Hindi, Spanish, French, German, Japanese and Simplified Chinese. The language is passed to the model as a hint, and it is also used when OCR'ing scanned PDF pages.

What exactly leaves my computer?

The image you selected — cropped, resized and JPEG-encoded — plus the OCR prompt and your API key, sent directly to the provider you chose. If you sign in, your email address and encrypted history entries go to your own Firebase project.

Is my history uploaded?

Only if you sign in. While signed out, history lives in chrome.storage.local. While signed in, entry text is encrypted on your device with AES-256-GCM before it is stored in your account's database, and deleting your account removes it.

Can I change models later?

Yes. Settings lists the free Gemini models plus the free vision models OpenRouter currently exposes, and the list refreshes when OpenRouter publishes new ones. Your key and model choice are remembered per device.
